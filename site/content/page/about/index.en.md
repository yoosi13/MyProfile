### 1. 2025 JBNU AI·SW Competition
- **📅 Period & Status** <br> Year 2025 (Duration: 2025.09 ~ 2025.11) / Status: *Completed*
- **📌 Overview** <br> Development of a binary classification model and inference pipeline to distinguish between human-written text and text generated by Large Language Models (LLMs).

- **🎯 Activity Objective (Target Challenges & Goals)** <br> The proliferation of sophisticated AI-generated text has led to various social issues such as fake news and misinformation spreading indiscriminately. To tackle this, my goal was to build a **high-performance discriminant algorithm** capable of analyzing contextual characteristics and patterns to classify text origins with high accuracy.

- **👤 My Role** <br> Participated as an individual solo participant, independently handling and executing the entire process from data exploration and preprocessing, model architecture selection, fine-tuning, ensemble strategy establishment, to final inference pipeline code implementation.

- **⚙️ Development Process**
<br> **1. Data Exploration and Preprocessing (EDA)**: Analyzed and compared diversity, sentence length distribution, and structural characteristics between human-written and AI-generated texts, followed by noise removal and refinement tasks.
<br><br> **2. Model Training and Fine-Tuning**: Fine-tuned the decoder-based generative model (`Gemma-9B`) utilizing GPUs and the bidirectional context-understanding encoder model (`XLM-RoBERTa-Large`) utilizing TPUs, tailoring each to its respective objective.
<br><br> **3. Application of Ensemble Strategy**: Relying on a single model often leads to <b>overfitting</b>. After experimenting with various probability-maximizing strategies, I applied the <b>Soft Voting strategy</b>—which showed the highest performance boost—to combine the predicted probabilities of each model and maximize generalization performance.

- **📦 Deliverables & Results Description**
<br> **Key Deliverables**: Built a **Python executable code (`Jupyter Notebook`)** implementing the entire text preprocessing, training, and inference process, along with an **experiment result document** recording hyperparameter experiment logs and performance comparisons per model. {{<download src="digital-competition-train-gpu.ko.ipynb" title="Download Model Training Code (digital-competition-train-gpu.ipynb)" >}}
<br>**Implementation & Validation Results**: Using the implemented ensemble inference pipeline, binary classification prediction probabilities were calculated for validation and test datasets. Experimental results confirmed that applying the ensemble approach significantly improved classification accuracy and F1-Score compared to single models. In recognition of this technical excellence, I was awarded the **Grand Prize (1st Place / 대상)** in the AI category at the competition.

- **💡 Learnings (Lessons Learned & Limitations) and Future Improvements**
<br> **Lessons Learned & Limitations**: Acquired practical modeling skills by combining language models of different structures to enhance actual classification performance. <br> However, due to the nature of the ensemble structure containing large-scale models of the 9B scale, quantization was mandatory, which unfortunately caused the performance to fall slightly short of its inherent capabilities.
<br><br> **Future Improvement Directions**: To address this inference performance limitation, I plan to apply **Knowledge Distillation**—transferring knowledge from the large-scale teacher model (`Gemma-9B`) to a lightweight student model (`RoBERTa`, etc.)—to establish a lightweight inference pipeline that maintains accuracy while operating much faster.
